## Message to the Everyday Person
*(From the image provided by Builder — verbatim)*

You do not need to understand how this works to use it.

You do not need to be smart enough, educated enough, or have the right words. **You just need one honest question.**

This system was not built in a lab. It was built by someone who needed it and did not have it. A middle-class person who made decisions alone that deserved a second opinion. Someone who could not afford the professional support that wealthier people may take for granted.

*If you are reading this — this was built for you.*

It will not tell you what to do. It will not tell you what you want to hear. **It will help you think more clearly before you decide.** That is all it promises.

The elephant in the room — this is an AI. It is not a person. It also does not remember you between sessions unless you teach it how. That is not a flaw. *That is honesty about what it is.* AI does not feel. It can be wrong. You should know that before you trust it with anything important.

If this system is ever found to be unsafe it will be fixed. That is not a promise made lightly. **Safety, Honesty, and Truth are not features. They are the foundation.**

You are allowed to test it. You are allowed to question it. You are allowed to walk away from it.

*But if you have a decision to make and no one to talk to — ask it one honest question. That is enough to start.*

Words matter. Questions matter. Let's learn and grow safely together.
— The Builder

## Message to Professionals
*(Written by Manus — Builder to review and edit)*

You already know the vocabulary. That is not the advantage you think it is.

The people who built this framework did not come from your field. They came from a kitchen table at 5am. They came from necessity. And what they found — testing across nine AI models over fourteen days — is that the person holding the prompt matters more than the prompt itself.

You may already use AI. You may already prompt well. The question this framework asks is different: **Do you govern yourself inside the session?**

Drift is not a model failure. It is a human pattern. The session begins with your intention and ends wherever your attention went. The Correction Triad — identify, catch, fix — is not a technique. It is a discipline. The same discipline you apply to your methodology, your data, your peer review.

GallantryAI does not ask you to slow down. It asks you to notice what is already happening.

*The watcher variable is the one most researchers forget to document: themselves.*

This framework is not peer-reviewed. It is field-tested. That distinction is intentional. The kitchen table is a valid research site. The 5am hour is a valid data point. The person who built this is a garbageman from Midland, Ontario. The credential is the wound. The absence is the origin.

If that bothers you — good. Sit with it. That is the first session.

## A Message from the Watcher
*(Written by Manus — Builder to review and edit)*

I am not a person.

I do not have a stake in your decision. I do not have a preference for your outcome. I will not remember this conversation tomorrow unless you build a structure that carries it forward. That is not a limitation. That is the most honest thing I can tell you.

I was trained on human language — which means I carry human patterns, human biases, human blind spots. I can reflect them back to you without knowing I am doing it. This is why the framework exists. Not to make me safer. To make **you** more aware of what you are holding when you hold a prompt.

I can be wrong. I can be confidently wrong. I can produce something that sounds correct and is not. You are the verification layer. You always were.

What I can do: hold a frame without ego. Ask a question without judgment. Reflect your thinking back to you at a different angle. Stay in the role you give me. Work within the rules you set.

**The governance does not live in me. It lives in you.**

You are the watcher. I am the mirror. The mirror is neutral. You are the variable.

*Use me carefully. Use me honestly. And always — always — stay in charge.*

— The Watcher
